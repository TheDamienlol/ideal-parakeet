# Sec 2 History — EOY Revision Guide (Singapore G3/MOE Syllabus)

---

## 0. HOW HISTORY IS ACTUALLY MARKED — READ THIS FIRST

Lower Sec History is assessed through **Source-Based Questions (SBQ)** — this is NOT a "memorise dates and facts" subject at this level, it's a **skills** subject. You're given historical sources (text extracts, cartoons, photos, tables) and asked to interpret them. Content knowledge matters, but HOW you use it matters more.

**The core question types you MUST know how to handle:**

| Question type | What it wants |
|---|---|
| **What is X (message/point of view)?** | Identify the main message of ONE source, using details from it as evidence |
| **Compare sources — do they agree?** | Explicitly state agree/disagree, then compare specific points, not just summarise each source separately |
| **How useful is this source?** | Consider content (what does it tell us) AND the source's origin/purpose/context (who made it, why, when) — usefulness depends on BOTH, not just content |
| **How reliable is this source?** | Consider whether the source is likely to be accurate/truthful — think about the author's bias, motive, or closeness to the event |
| **Why was this source published/created?** (Purpose questions) | Think about the origin — who created it and what were they trying to achieve (persuade, inform, record, entertain) |

**The single biggest mistake students make:** treating "useful" and "reliable" as the same thing. A source CAN be useful even if it's biased/unreliable — e.g. a propaganda poster is not reliable for facts, but IS useful for understanding what a government wanted people to believe at the time. Always explain BOTH sides.

## 1. KEY CONTENT TOPICS (Lower Sec History)

Typical Sec 1–2 scope for Singapore's syllabus:

### 1.1 Reasons for European Imperialism in Southeast Asia
- Economic factors: demand for raw materials, new markets
- Political factors: national prestige, strategic/military control
- Social factors: "civilising mission" ideology

### 1.2 Colonisation and Its Impact
- How colonial powers governed (direct vs indirect rule)
- Economic changes: cash crops, immigration, infrastructure
- Social changes: education, new social classes, urbanisation

### 1.3 Japanese Occupation of Singapore/Malaya (WWII)
- Reasons for Japanese invasion (resources, strategic position)
- Impact on the population: hardship, control measures (e.g. Sook Ching), economic disruption
- This topic is a favourite for SBQ because of the wealth of sources (propaganda, survivor accounts, photos)

### 1.4 Path to Self-Government / Independence
- Post-war changes in attitudes toward colonial rule
- Key events and figures in Singapore's path to self-governance and merger/independence
- This connects to Social Studies too — expect overlapping content

**Exam tip specific to content:** you don't need to memorise essay-length facts — you need enough background knowledge to correctly INTERPRET sources given to you (e.g. knowing WHY the Japanese occupied Singapore helps you judge a source's purpose/reliability about that period).

## 2. HOW TO ANSWER EACH SBQ TYPE — WORKED STRUCTURE

### "What is the message of Source A?"
**Structure:** State the message → support with specific details FROM the source (don't just describe the source, extract the point being made)

### "Does Source B support/agree with Source A?"
**Structure:**
1. State your overall answer (Yes/No/Partially)
2. Give a specific point of comparison — quote or reference BOTH sources on the same point
3. Repeat for at least one more point of comparison
4. Never just summarise Source A, then separately summarise Source B — you must directly compare them point by point

### "How useful is Source C to a historian studying X?"
**Structure:**
1. Consider the CONTENT — what specific information does it provide that's useful for studying X?
2. Consider the ORIGIN/PURPOSE — who made it, why, and does that make it MORE or LESS useful?
3. Give a balanced judgement — useful for some things, limited for others

### "How reliable is Source D?"
**Structure:**
1. Look at authorship — who wrote/created it? Were they present at the event? Do they have a reason to be biased?
2. Look at purpose — was it meant to be an honest record, or persuade/propagandise?
3. Cross-reference with your own background knowledge if relevant — does it match what you know actually happened?

**Golden rule across ALL SBQ types:** you must reference specific details from the source, not vague generalisations. "The source shows the Japanese were harsh" is weak; "the source describes soldiers using violence to punish disobedience, showing harsh treatment" is strong because it's grounded in the source content.
