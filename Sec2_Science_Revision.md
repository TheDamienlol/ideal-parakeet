## 0. HOW SINGAPORE SCIENCE EXAMS ACTUALLY WORK

Before content — this is the part most notes skip, and it's why you lose marks even when you "know the answer."

**Command words = how much you need to write:**

| Command word | What it wants |
|---|---|
| **State / Name / Give** | One short fact. No explanation needed. 1 mark = 1 point. |
| **Define** | The exact textbook-style meaning of a term. |
| **List** | Points, no elaboration. |
| **Describe** | What happens, in sequence/detail — but NOT why. |
| **Explain** | The WHY / mechanism. This is where most marks are lost — students describe instead of explain. |
| **Suggest** | No fixed answer in the syllabus — apply your knowledge to a new (unseen) scenario. |
| **Compare** | Must mention BOTH items, usually using words like "whereas" / "but" / "while". |
| **Calculate** | Show working — formula, substitution, answer with units. Even if final answer is wrong, working gets method marks. |

**Golden rule:** count the marks allocated. 3 marks = examiner wants 3 distinct points. If you write one long sentence for a 3-mark "explain" question, you're leaving marks on the table.

**Common losing habits:**
- Writing "it moves faster" instead of "the speed increases" (avoid vague words like "it", "moves better", "stronger")
- Forgetting units in calculations
- Not linking back to the actual context/scenario given in the question (Singapore exams love applying concepts to weird real-life scenarios — you must use their scenario, not a generic textbook answer)

## 1. CHEMISTRY

### 1.1 Matter, Elements, Compounds, Mixtures

**Key facts:**
- Matter = anything with mass and takes up space. Three states: solid, liquid, gas.
- **Element**: pure substance, cannot be broken down further chemically, made of ONE type of atom.
- **Compound**: two or more elements chemically combined in a FIXED ratio; properties differ completely from the elements that made it.
- **Mixture**: two or more substances physically combined, NOT in fixed ratio; each substance keeps its own properties; can be separated by physical means.

**Common exam trap:** "Explain why a compound is different from a mixture" — expected answer must mention (1) fixed ratio vs variable ratio, (2) chemical bond vs no bond, (3) new properties vs retained properties, (4) compounds need chemical means to separate, mixtures need physical means.

### 1.2 Separation Techniques — MUST memorise which technique for which situation

| Technique | Used for | Key principle |
|---|---|---|
| Filtration | Insoluble solid from liquid | Particle size — solid too big to pass through filter paper |
| Evaporation | Dissolved solid from solution (when you only want the solid) | Heat drives off liquid, solid remains |
| Crystallisation | Dissolved solid from solution (when solid decomposes on strong heating, e.g. sugar) | Gentle heating + cooling forms crystals |
| Distillation | Liquid from solution (want the liquid back), OR two miscible liquids with different boiling points | Different boiling points |
| Separating funnel | Two immiscible liquids (e.g. oil and water) | Different densities, don't mix |
| Magnetic separation | Magnetic solid from non-magnetic solid mixture | Magnetism |
| Sublimation | Substance that sublimes (e.g. iodine) from a mixture | Turns solid directly to gas |

**Typical exam question:** "You are given a mixture of sand, salt, and iron filings, all in solid form. Describe how you would separate this mixture into its three components." (This is a classic — expects a *sequence*: magnet first for iron filings → add water to dissolve salt, filter for sand → evaporate/crystallise filtrate for salt.)

### 1.3 Physical vs Chemical Change

- **Physical change**: no new substance formed, usually reversible (melting, dissolving, evaporating).
- **Chemical change**: new substance(s) formed, usually irreversible, signs include: colour change, gas produced (bubbles), temperature change, precipitate (solid) formed.

**Exam trap:** Students confuse "gas produced" from boiling (physical — it's steam, same substance) vs gas produced from a reaction (chemical — different substance, e.g. bubbles when acid + metal). Always ask: is it the SAME substance in a different state?

## 2. BIOLOGY

### 2.1 Cells

- Cell theory: all living things made of cells; cell = basic unit of life.
- **Plant cell vs Animal cell** — classic compare question:

| Structure | Plant cell | Animal cell |
|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | Present (cellulose) | Absent |
| Chloroplast | Present | Absent |
| Vacuole | Large, permanent | Small/absent |
| Shape | Fixed, regular | Irregular |
| Nucleus | Present | Present |

**Exam phrasing to expect:** "Fig X shows a cell observed under a microscope. State, with reasons, whether it is a plant or animal cell." → You must state the feature AND explain why that feature indicates plant/animal (not just name the cell type).

### 2.2 Photosynthesis & Nutrition

**Word equation (must know cold):**
carbon dioxide + water --(light energy, chlorophyll)--> glucose + oxygen

**Key facts:**
- Occurs in chloroplasts (contains chlorophyll, which absorbs light energy)
- Factors affecting rate: light intensity, CO₂ concentration, temperature (these are "limiting factors")
- Test for starch: iodine solution turns blue-black if starch present (confirms photosynthesis occurred)

**Classic experiment question:** "A variegated leaf (part green, part white) was tested for starch. Explain the result." → Expected: only the green parts (with chlorophyll) turn blue-black because only those parts can photosynthesise; white parts lack chlorophyll so cannot photosynthesise, no starch produced there.

### 2.3 Human Digestive System

- Sequence: mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → rectum → anus
- **Mechanical digestion** = physical breakdown (chewing, churning) — increases surface area
- **Chemical digestion** = enzymes breaking down large molecules into small ones
- Key enzymes: amylase (starch → sugar, in mouth & small intestine), protease (protein → amino acids, stomach & small intestine), lipase (fats → fatty acids + glycerol, small intestine)
- Small intestine absorbs nutrients — villi increase surface area for absorption

**Exam trap:** "Explain why the small intestine is long and has villi" — must connect structure to FUNCTION: increases surface area → increases rate/efficiency of absorption. Just saying "so it can absorb more" without the surface area link loses marks.

### 2.4 Respiratory System

- Pathway: nose → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli
- Alveoli adapted for gas exchange: thin walls (short diffusion distance), large surface area, moist, good blood supply
- **Breathing (mechanical) ≠ Respiration (chemical, happens in cells, releases energy from glucose)** — huge common confusion, examiners test this deliberately

Word equation for aerobic respiration:
glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water (+ energy)

### 2.5 Transport System (Circulatory)

- Heart pumps blood; double circulation (to lungs, then to body)
- Blood vessels: arteries (away from heart, thick walls, high pressure), veins (to heart, thinner walls, valves to prevent backflow), capillaries (exchange of substances, one-cell thick walls)
- Blood components: red blood cells (carry oxygen via haemoglobin), white blood cells (fight infection), platelets (clotting), plasma (transport medium)

### 2.6 Reproduction

- Human reproductive systems (male/female organs and functions)
- Menstrual cycle basics, fertilisation, pregnancy stages (topic depth varies by school — flag to your teacher if unsure how deep you need to go)
- Plant reproduction: parts of a flower and their functions (sepal, petal, stamen, pistil), pollination vs fertilisation (these are NOT the same thing — classic exam trap)

## 3. PHYSICS

### 3.1 Forces

- Force = push or pull; measured in Newtons (N)
- Types: gravitational, friction, normal, applied, tension, upthrust
- **Turning effect of forces (moments):** Moment = Force × perpendicular distance from pivot
  - Principle of moments: for equilibrium, sum of clockwise moments = sum of anticlockwise moments
- **Pressure:** Pressure = Force ÷ Area (P = F/A), units: Pascals (Pa) or N/m²
  - Smaller area = higher pressure for same force (why knives are sharp, why snowshoes work)

**Typical calculation question:** Given force and area, calculate pressure — remember units, and remember to convert cm² to m² if needed (common careless mistake).

### 3.2 Energy, Work, Power

- Energy forms: kinetic, potential (gravitational/elastic), chemical, thermal, electrical, light, sound — and conversions between them (e.g. a torch: chemical → electrical → light)
- Principle of conservation of energy: energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted from one form to another
- Work done = Force × distance moved in direction of force (W = F × d)
- Power = Work done ÷ time (P = W/t)

**Exam trap:** "Explain the energy conversion when a ball is thrown upward and falls back down" — must trace through ALL stages: chemical (muscles) → kinetic (thrown) → kinetic decreases, gravitational PE increases as it rises → at highest point, all KE converted to GPE → GPE converts back to KE as it falls.

### 3.3 Electricity & Magnetism

- Current (A), voltage/p.d. (V), resistance (Ω) — Ohm's Law: V = IR
- Series circuit: same current throughout, voltage splits across components
- Parallel circuit: same voltage across each branch, current splits
- Magnetism: like poles repel, unlike poles attract; magnetic field lines go from N to S outside the magnet
- Electromagnet: current-carrying wire produces a magnetic field; can be strengthened by more coils, more current, or an iron core
