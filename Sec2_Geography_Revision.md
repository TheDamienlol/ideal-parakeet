## 0. HOW GEOGRAPHY IS MARKED

Like History, Lower Sec Geography leans heavily on **applying concepts to given data/diagrams/case studies**, not pure memorisation. Common question types: describe (a pattern/trend from a map, graph or photo), explain (the geographical process behind it), and suggest (apply knowledge to a new scenario).

**Command word discipline matters just as much here as in Science:**
- **Describe** = what the data/map SHOWS (trend, pattern, location) — no reasons needed
- **Explain** = the geographical PROCESS or REASON behind the pattern
- **Suggest** = apply your understanding to a scenario not explicitly taught — there's no single "correct" answer, examiners want logical geographical reasoning

**Common mistake:** mixing description and explanation together, or explaining when only asked to describe (wastes time) — or worse, describing when asked to explain (loses marks because you never actually answered why).

## 1. KEY CONTENT TOPICS (Lower Sec Geography)

### 1.1 Map Skills & Map Reading
- Grid references (4-figure vs 6-figure), scale & distance calculation, direction/bearings
- Contour lines — reading relief/elevation, identifying steep vs gentle slopes (closely spaced contours = steep)
- Cross-sections

**Common error:** 6-figure grid references — remember "along the corridor, up the stairs" (read the EASTING/horizontal number first, then the NORTHING/vertical number).

### 1.2 Weather & Climate
- Difference between weather (short-term, day-to-day) and climate (long-term average pattern)
- Elements of weather: temperature, rainfall, humidity, wind
- Singapore's equatorial climate — high consistent temperature, high rainfall, high humidity all year, linked to its position near the equator (direct overhead sun)

### 1.3 Tectonic Processes (Earthquakes & Volcanoes)
- Plate boundaries: divergent (plates moving apart), convergent (plates colliding), transform (plates sliding past)
- Different boundary types cause different hazards — e.g. convergent boundaries often cause volcanic activity AND earthquakes, transform boundaries mainly cause earthquakes
- Impact of earthquakes/volcanic eruptions: primary effects (immediate, e.g. ground shaking, lava) vs secondary effects (knock-on, e.g. tsunamis, disease, economic loss)

**Exam trap:** confusing "cause" (the tectonic process) with "impact" (the human/environmental consequence) — these are usually asked as separate question parts, don't mix them.

### 1.4 Rivers / Coasts (varies by school — some do rivers, some do coasts, some both)
- River processes: erosion, transportation, deposition
- Landforms created by each process at different stages of a river's course
- If coasts: wave action (constructive vs destructive waves), erosion/deposition landforms (headlands, bays, beaches, spits)

## 2. HOW TO ANSWER TYPICAL GEOGRAPHY EXAM QUESTIONS

**"Describe the distribution/pattern shown in Fig X"**
Structure: overall pattern first (e.g. "concentrated in the north," "increasing trend over time") → THEN specific supporting details/figures from the map or graph, with actual numbers/locations where possible.

**"Explain why [geographical phenomenon] occurs"**
Structure: state the process → explain the mechanism step by step → link explicitly back to the specific case/location in the question, not just a generic textbook explanation.

**"Suggest reasons for..."**
This is an APPLICATION question — take the geographical concepts you know and reason through the NEW scenario logically. Examiners accept multiple valid answers as long as the reasoning is geographically sound.

**Using command terms correctly in your OWN answer matters too** — use precise geographical vocabulary (e.g. "erosion," "gradient," "precipitation") rather than vague everyday words ("wears away," "steepness," "rain").
